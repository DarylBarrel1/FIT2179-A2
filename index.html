Part 02 — Southeast Asia
How Does Australia Compare to Southeast Asia?

Southeast Asia is one of the most diverse regions in the world, not just culturally, but in how its people access healthcare. From Singapore's world-class hospitals to the Philippines' stretched public system, the gap between the best and worst resourced countries is explored. Understanding these differences is the first step to examine why life expectancy varies so dramatically across the region.

The relationship between public health spending and life expectancy isn't as simple as we expected. Countries that dedicate a higher share of their budget to healthcare tends to have a higher life expectancy, but Singapore is a notable exception. They spend less publicly than Australia yet achieves comparable life expectancy. Additionally, how a country spends its healthcare budget and how efficiently its system delivers care matters just as much as how much it spends.

Physicians per 1,000 by country (2021) Our World in Data
Life expectancy vs Public Health Expenditure (2023) Our World in Data

Across Southeast Asia, the story of healthcare is one of gradual but uneven progress. Every country in the region has improved its life expectancy since 1970, a testament to decades of investment in better nutrition, sanitation, vaccination and medical access. Yet the gains have not been equal. Singapore has pulled far ahead, building a healthcare system that rivals the best in the world. Meanwhile, countries like the Philippines and Indonesia with populations in the tens of millions spread across thousands of islands continue to struggle with critical shortages of doctors, hospitals and funding. With fewer than two physicians per 1,000 people, millions of patients in these countries face long distances, long waits and limited options when they fall ill. The challenge for Southeast Asia is not just economic, it is structural. Building the infrastructure, training the workforce and funding the systems needed to close these gaps will take decades of sustained commitment.

Part 03 — The Connection
Does investing more in healthcare actually save lives?

The bubble chart makes it clear that countries with more doctors per person consistently achieve higher life expectancy. Australia and Singapore sit at the top which contrasts Philippines, where it is located on the opposing end with fewer physicians and lower spending and the shortest lives in the region. Additionally, the dot plot confirms the ranking, reinforcing our claim over the bubble chart.

Countries that invest more in healthcare tend to have a higher life-expectancy. However, certain countries such as Singapore and Thailand demonstrates that efficiency and quality of care are additional factors needing to be considered. Australia leads the region not just because it spends more, but because it has built a system that produces high quality health care.

For Southeast Asia, the challenge is not simply spending more, but having a more effective spending while ensuring that the investment reaches the communities that need it most.
